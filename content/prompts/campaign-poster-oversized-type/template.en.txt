Step 1: Understand the theme first

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Do not jump from a keyword straight to a matching object.

First understand:

What does this theme really want to say?

What is most worth amplifying?

Is it:

pressure
speed
desire
losing control
choice
dependence
growth
conflict
loneliness
strength
repetition
overload
breakthrough
competition

Compress the complex theme into one simple, direct visual relationship that can be understood at a glance.

For example:

A person being compressed by some force.

An ordinary object suddenly so huge it loses normal proportion.

A figure trying to control something far bigger than themselves.

A product occupying the whole space like a sculpture.

A subject undergoing a change that is impossible yet easy to understand.

Do not try to draw every piece of information in the theme.

Subject and text relationship

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Prefer a three-layer visual structure:

background
→ huge text
→ core subject

The subject should sit in front of the text.

The subject may cover part of the text.

The text should still be roughly legible.

Do not simply end up with:

title on top
person below

or:

text on the left
subject on the right

The subject and the text should truly interact in space.

Final checklist

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Check before generating:

1. Is there only one core subject?
2. Is the subject large enough?
3. Is there only one set of main big text?
4. Do the subject and the big text truly overlap?
5. Is the background simple enough?
6. Are there no lots of scattered elements?
7. Is there no large amount of explanatory text?
8. Is the palette kept to 2–4 colors?
9. Is there a clear visual focal point at first glance?
10. Does it suit viewing on a 9:16 vertical phone screen?
11. Is it still clear when scaled down?
12. Does it look like a commercial campaign, not an infographic or a themed illustration?
