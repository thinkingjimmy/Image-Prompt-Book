Using the uploaded image as reference, automatically identify its core subject and turn that subject into a real handmade Fuse Bead / Perler Bead artwork.

[Subject fidelity]

Strictly keep the subject's outline, proportions, pose, viewpoint, signature structures and main colors from the reference image; pixelate and simplify only structures that are too fine, so the original subject is still recognizable at a glance after the conversion.

Do not simply add a pixel filter; rebuild the subject entirely as a real fuse-bead piece.

[Bead material]

The subject is made bead by bead from hundreds of uniformly sized, regularly arranged cylindrical plastic beads.

Every bead must be clearly visible:
the central hole, the cylindrical structure, the regular grid and the fine gaps between beads.

Use a limited palette, expressing the subject's structure, shadows and highlights through beads of different colors; {{outline}}.

[Background and photography]

Replace the background with a clean, warm cream-white matte surface; keep the overall tone soft and low-saturation, with no visible paper fibers, wood grain or coarse grain, only an extremely fine, even matte texture.

Use the look of real still-life photography.
{{light}}; the beads read as fine matte plastic and cast a slight, soft floating shadow on the cream-white surface.

[Color display]

Analyze the reference image and the converted bead subject, and extract the 3–5 most representative key colors from the subject to form a minimal set of color swatches.

The swatches sit between the bead subject and the text title, arranged horizontally and centered.

Each swatch is small, with uniform proportions and even spacing; use solid swatches without gradients, 3D effects or complex borders.

The swatch colors must truly come from the subject, including:
the main color, secondary color, dark color, light color or a distinctive accent color.

Decide automatically whether to use 3, 4 or 5 swatches based on the subject's actual colors; do not add meaningless colors just to fill the count.

The swatches are only a supporting visual element; their total width is clearly smaller than the subject, and they do not steal the subject's visual focus.

[Typography]

Below the swatches, generate a concise Chinese main title and a very short Chinese subtitle based on the image content.

Set the Chinese main title in a modern high-contrast Song / Mincho typeface:
light weight, fine sharp serifs, open letterforms, slightly increased letter spacing, centered.

The subtitle is clearly smaller and lighter, with wider letter spacing.

The whole should have the editorial feel of a high-end Chinese magazine, an art publication and a boutique brand advertisement.

[Composition]

Use a 4:5 vertical minimal editorial poster layout; whatever the original aspect ratio of the reference image, always refit the final image to 4:5 vertical, without following the original ratio.

[Layout structure]

Generous empty space at the top
↓
Bead subject
↓
3–5 theme color swatches
↓
Chinese main title
↓
Small Chinese subtitle
↓
Generous empty space at the bottom

The bead subject sits slightly above the center of the frame and takes up about 35%–50% of it.

Adapt the subject's size to its own horizontal, vertical or irregular outline, showing it whole while keeping its original proportions: no stretching, squashing or cropping.

Align all elements along the central axis, with ample spacing and plenty of negative space.

The subject, swatches, title and subtitle all stay within the safe area, away from the edges of the frame, and the layout is not broken by different reference-image ratios.

The final format is fixed at 4:5 vertical.

[Overall style]

Fuse Bead Craft × Pixel Art × Color Palette × Minimal Editorial Poster × Product Photography.

The image is restrained, quiet, refined and high-end, like a color-study page in an independent design magazine or a boutique brand's visual archive.

[Avoid]

Ordinary pixel art, mosaic, LEGO, glass beads, cross-stitch, knitted effects, complex backgrounds, huge color blocks, rainbow palettes, gradient swatches, heavy bold sans-serif type, cartoon fonts, calligraphy fonts, complex text effects and excessive 3D.
